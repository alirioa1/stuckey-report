Street Children Ministry
Juba, South Sudan · $41,000 Invested
Bread, Bandages, and the Long Work of Trust
The team goes to the markets where the children already are — Konyokonyo, Jebel, Nyakuron, Kubur Haboba, Gumbo — and they bring bread. While the children eat, the team cleans and dresses wounds, prays with them, and listens to their stories. They return every week, because a child who has been failed by adults does not trust one on the first visit. Septic wounds are the most common serious case: injuries left untreated long enough to turn dangerous. The quieter result this period was reunification — six young men who came to SCM as vulnerable boys were traced back to relatives, carefully prepared, and returned to their families, where they have become a support rather than a burden.

Call of Love Ministries
Iraq & the Middle East · $100,000 Invested
A Boy Named Medhat
He is sixteen. His father is a high-ranking Islamic leader in Iraq. He wrote to Glory TV under a false name, because using his own could have cost him everything. He had a great many questions. The contact centre team did not argue with him — they explained the difference between a relationship with Jesus, who offers salvation freely, and the rules and requirements of earning it through religion. That distinction was what undid him. Medhat prayed over the phone, with a stranger in another country, to accept Christ as his Saviour. He still lives in his father’s house, studies the Bible secretly through the Glory TV website, and told the team he is not battling anxiety anymore — that before a school exam, he prayed and asked for the Lord’s help.

A Child's Hope International — Aldea Xalcatá
$14,500 · A Project Clay Facilitated · Guatemala
First Delivery. 285,120 Meals to Come.
In Alta Verapaz — where 73% of families are food insecure and child malnutrition ranks among the worst in the Western Hemisphere — the hungry season arrived again this May. So did something new. On May 22, volunteers from Iglesia La Gracia made the first food delivery to Aldea Xalcatá: high-protein meals distributed to 62 families, who cooked together, ate together, and heard the gospel together. It was one community of eight — the beginning of a plan to deliver 285,120 meals across the region over the next 8 to 10 months, two distributions per month, with discipleship built into every visit. Clay helped bring this partnership to life. Because of your investment, the first meals have arrived. Tens of thousands more are on the way.
City Light Center / Julie's Heart Cry — Bangui, Central African Republic
Street Children Initiative · $400,000+ Invested
His Name Means “God Is Powerful” — and Now He Knows Why
His name is Dieu Puissant — “God is Powerful” in French. For nearly five years, that name felt more like a distant hope than a lived reality. After his parents separated, he left home and survived on the streets of Bangui, one of the most fragile cities in the world. Then the City Light Center team found him. Through faithful outreach, consistent care, and the patient sharing of the gospel, Dieu Puissant decided to go home. On May 18th, 2026, he placed his faith in Jesus Christ for the first time — understanding the gospel not just as something he’d heard, but as something he now believed. His restoration is physical, relational, and eternal. And he is one of many stories beginning to emerge through your investment in the Street Children Initiative.

Jesus.net — enormous reach, thinner follow-through
Views117.2M reported · 172.4M forecast
Discipleship253,672 forecast vs 380,000 baseline
Across both grants ($1.91M committed) reported views stand at 117.2M with a forecast of 172.4M, comfortably ahead of the combined 125M baseline. Yet active people in a journey are forecast at 253,672 against a combined 380,000 baseline. Combined touchpoints total 487,973.
Comparing discipleship across grantees — like for like
Palau124,669 signed up for follow-up
Jesus.net equivalent487,973 touchpoints reported · 845,010 forecast
Jesus.net actives168,675 at 7+ interactions
These three figures measure different things and should not be added together. Palau’s number counts people who signed up to be followed up. Jesus.net’s comparable measure is touchpoints — 487,973 reported to date across both projects combined, forecast to reach 845,010. Jesus.net’s “active people” figure is a far higher bar: someone who has interacted seven or more times. Read on the same basis, digital follow-up engagement is running roughly four times Palau’s sign-up volume.
