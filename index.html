Street Children Ministry
Juba, South Sudan · $41,000 Invested
Bread, Bandages, and the Long Work of Trust
The team goes to the markets where the children already are — Konyokonyo, Jebel, Nyakuron, Kubur Haboba, Gumbo — and they bring bread. While the children eat, the team cleans and dresses wounds, prays with them, and listens to their stories. They return every week, because a child who has been failed by adults does not trust one on the first visit. Septic wounds are the most common serious case: injuries left untreated long enough to turn dangerous. The quieter result this period was reunification — six young men who came to SCM as vulnerable boys were traced back to relatives, carefully prepared, and returned to their families, where they have become a support rather than a burden.

He is sixteen. His father is a high-ranking Islamic leader in Iraq. He wrote to Glory TV under a false name, because using his own could have cost him everything. He had a great many questions. A Child's Hope International — Aldea Xalcatá
$14,500 · A Project Clay Facilitated · Guatemala
First Delivery. 285,120 Meals to Come.
In Alta Verapaz — where 73% of families are food insecure and child malnutrition ranks among the worst in the Western Hemisphere — the hungry season arrived again this May. So did something new. On May 22, volunteers from Iglesia La Gracia made the first food delivery to Aldea Xalcatá: high-protein meals distributed to 62 families, who cooked together, ate together, and heard the gospel together. It was one community of eight — the beginning of a plan to deliver 285,120 meals across the region over the next 8 to 10 months, two distributions per month, with discipleship built into every visit. Clay helped bring this partnership to life. Because of your investment, the first meals have arrived. Tens of thousands more are on the way.

05 · Cost efficiency

Cost ratios

Portfolio-wide cost ratios as published for this reporting cycle. A falling figure is favourable — the outcome costs less than planned.

Why there is no forecast cost per decision

IGI records professions of faith that ministries report, and it does not project them forward. That is a deliberate position, not a gap in the data: a decision belongs to the person who made it and to God, and modelling future conversions as a planning output would misrepresent what this work is.

Baseline decision figures exist because grantees set them, and reported decisions stand at 28.02 million against an 18.53 million baseline, or 151%. Read that as progress through live projects rather than as a shortfall — 51 of the 84 projects have not yet reported at all.

The two outcomes IGI does forecast are both ahead: reach +28.1% and discipleship journeys +-8.5% against baseline.

How IGI attribution works

  • Each project’s attribution is the lesser of 100% and (IGI dollars + dollars matched) ÷ total project cost. People reached through your giving = attribution × the reported full-project figure. In the data this is called IGI attribution.
  • A match is credited only where one is indicated, and generally up to 1:1 with IGI’s dollars. A match below 1:1 is credited in proportion.
  • Exceptions: Mailbox Club, $400K counted as dollars matched. Luis Palau Association and the GO Viral waves, 1:1. Close the Gaps and CEF Children Reaching Children, 100%. The Timothy Initiative, 4:1 — IGI’s $200K mobilized another $200K given to IGI to grant, and TTI matched $400K. Achieve (TTI), ICM Cambodia and ICM Burundi are credited in full, not capped.
  • GO Movement 2026: 63% = (IGI’s $800K + GO’s $800K match) ÷ GO’s $2,539,100 budget.
  • Forecast against baseline, and cost per person, come out the same for each project under both views, because attribution scales a project’s promise, results and forecast by the same share. Project performance and cost ratios are therefore shown on the full project.
  • The dollars-only view counts IGI dollars alone (IGI dollars ÷ total cost) and is the most conservative reading.

Innovations for Gospel Impact

IGI finds, fuels, and funds gospel-proclaiming ministries. This portfolio belongs to the ministries in it — International Care Ministries, Child Evangelism Fellowship, Favor International, Jesus.net, GO Movement, the Luis Palau Association, Citylight Church, Every Orphan’s Hope, Alliance Care Now, Mailbox Club, The Timothy Initiative, Lon Solomon Ministries, Call of Love, American Bible Society, Generation Unashamed, Under Cover Pastor, Make Your Mark, Street Kids Initiative and their partners — and to the Stuckey family, whose giving made the work possible.

Source & method
IGI Reach 200M+ variance portfolio, covering projects from November 2024 through November 2026 (IGI Variance Portfolio Google Sheet, November 24, 2026 version). Portfolio money and outcome totals are the sheet’s own totals row. Project-level outcome figures are reconciled to the published monthly report and are therefore carried at its precision.

Baseline, reported and forecast figures are supplied by grantee ministries and reviewed by IGI. Forecasts are progress-based projections to project completion, not guarantees. Cost-per-outcome figures use total project cost. Professions of faith are reported only and are never forecast.

Five internal lines (two giving accounts, IGI operations, a grantee training program and an internal Boost fund) are excluded from the project views and charts; portfolio money totals include them.
